BEST DETECTION OF SNMP is through nmap first
it's usually on port 161, but might be elsewhere
it's a UDP service, so watch out, as you will have to do -sU flag

snmp is currently at snmpv3 which adds authentication and encryption; to make sure it's standardized, the management information base MIB was created; to footprint the service, tools like snmpwalk, onesixtyone, and braa are used; an OID represents a node in the hierarchical namespace,

a community string in snmp is like a password

you can use onesixtyone to brute force a community string:
onesixtyone -c <wordlist.txt> <target-ip>

then after finding the community string, use braa to brute force individual OIDs
braa <community-string>@<target-ip>:.1.3.4.*

you can enumerate using:
snmpwalk -v2c -c <community-string> <target-ip>
